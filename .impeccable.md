# DeltCapital — Design Context

## Users

U.S. small-to-mid business operators looking for fast merchant capital — restaurant owners, construction firms, salons, retail, auto repair, transportation. They are time-poor, often turned down by banks, frustrated by paperwork, and need money in days, not weeks. They visit on phone or laptop between shifts, often skeptical of fintech. They care about clarity, speed, and trust.

Job to be done: get a real funding offer fast, with no credit impact and no theater. Product surfaces:
- Marketing pages (Home, About, How It Works, Reviews, Wins, FAQ, Blog, Resources)
- Pre-qualification game / calculator (multi-step)
- Lead capture / "Get Funded" application flow
- Dashboard for funded merchants

## Brand Personality

Three words: **trustworthy, fast, transparent.**

Voice: precise, operator-friendly, anti-bureaucracy. Avoids hype copy, emojis, and "disrupting the industry" cliches. Says the dollar amount up front. Reads like a no-nonsense funding partner, not a Silicon Valley landing page.

Emotional goals: confidence (this is bank-grade), relief (no paperwork pain), urgency (same-day funding) — never anxiety, never doubt.

## Aesthetic Direction

Atlassian-inspired token base (per `design_spec.md`): primary `#0c66e4`, navy `#172b4d`, accent purple `#6e5dc6`, page bg `#fafbfc`. Inter as the primary type face, with Codec Pro / Open Sauce Sans as fallbacks for the existing Figma assets.

Light mode is the default for all marketing surfaces. Dark navy bands (`#172b4d`) are used for emphasis sections (PreQual, How-It-Works steps, ComparisonTable Delt column, Footer).

References that inform direction:
- **Atlassian Design System** — for tokens, hairlines, restraint
- **Linear / Vercel** — for glass surfaces, modern type, magnetic interactions
- **Stripe** — for trust-forward fintech tone

**Anti-references / what to avoid:**
- ❌ Bright neon-on-dark "AI cyber" aesthetics
- ❌ Dramatic purple→blue gradients on every surface (currently overused — see "Gradient retraction" below)
- ❌ Generic SaaS templated feel (rounded card grids, identical icon-above-heading patterns)
- ❌ Hype copy ("revolutionary," "game-changing," "AI-powered")
- ❌ Fake urgency theater (countdown timers, scarcity tags)

## Design Principles

1. **Restraint signals trust.** A funding product loses money when users hesitate. Every gradient, glow, and decoration has to earn its place; if it doesn't increase trust, cut it.
2. **Numbers do the talking.** Dollar amounts, time-to-fund, factor rates — these are the most important elements on any screen. Type weight + size + tabular-nums gives them prominence; we don't need decoration on top.
3. **Hierarchy through weight + space, not color.** Use one accent color sparingly. Most contrast should come from typographic weight, size, and breathing room — not from gradient text or colored backgrounds.
4. **Hairlines and surfaces over auras.** Prefer crisp 1px borders, soft single shadows, and clean fills over multiple stacked glows, mesh halos, and gradient rings. Light hand wins.
5. **Motion is functional, not decorative.** Reveals on scroll-in are fine. Pulsing dots, animated mesh gradients, and "ambient" loops belong only where they communicate live state (e.g., the live-payments dashboard's processing indicator).

## Gradient Retraction (current pass)

The redesign accumulated heavy gradient usage that needs to come down: `.bg-mesh` halos behind sections, `.text-gradient-primary` on most headlines, gradient ring borders via mask-composite on cards, gradient CTA buttons everywhere, gradient backgrounds on the comparison Delt column, gradient navbar pill, gradient stepper segments, gradient halo glows around buttons. The goal is to keep the trust-forward bolder voice but **switch most gradients out for solid Atlassian primary `#0c66e4` + restraint** — gradients survive only where they actually communicate something (live state, the single hero moment).
